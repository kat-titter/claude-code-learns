# Science design exemplars

Finished science pieces that a reader held up as good design, each read in the original and broken into what to take
and what to leave. work-practice §2 asks for three praised examples before a new form; for mechanism figures,
explainers and science booklets, start here, and add each new one the same way: what it is, how it was read, what to
adopt, what not to copy. Take the form and check the claims separately: a well-designed page can be weakly sourced.

## Mode-of-action booklet for a brain-injury drug (Cerebrolysin, EVER Pharma, 2021)

<https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/MoA_booklet_CEREINT092021-21.pdf>

52 landscape pages (16:9, 918 × 522 pt), set in InDesign, code CEREINT092021-21. It is the print companion to a
mode-of-action animation; the cover reads "Mode of Action / Booklet to video". Read 2026-11-22: the text layer, every
page at 40 dpi, four pages at 90 dpi.

**Adopt**

- **One scene, two media.** Each figure is a 1896 × 1080 frame from the animation, and each chapter opener carries a QR
  code to its part of the video. The booklet and the film share every asset, so a reader moving between them meets the
  same cells. For an explainer with a moving or interactive part, take the stills from that scene rather than redraw.
- **The camera moves inward.** The figures run in order of scale: the infused drug in the head and body (Figure 1), the
  vessel lumen with blood cells (2), the neurovascular unit (3), a thrombus (4), microbleeds and swollen endothelium
  (6 to 11), then neurons and glia (12 to 20). The order tells the reader where each view sits, with no inset.
- **A figure page faces a text page.** 20 full-bleed figures in 47 pages. A figure page holds 4 to 24 words (number,
  title, labels), the facing text page 40 to 280, and the text points back with "(see Figure N)" in the accent blue,
  17 times. Prose sits on a render only once, on a blurred continuation of Figure 3 (p. 7).
- **Labels live in their own column.** On the labelled figures a dark translucent band covers the right third of the
  render and the labels sit left-aligned inside it. Each leader leaves its label horizontally, turns at most once, and
  ends in an arrowhead on its object (Figures 2, 14, 18, 20). The render stays clean and the labels read as a list.
- **Leaders can cross the spine, so the text is the legend.** Figure 3's six leaders run across the gutter to six
  glossary entries, one cell type each with a rule between, set on the blurred render (pp. 6 to 7). Figure 13's three
  run to the three bullets on the facing white page (pp. 32 to 33).
- **The drug is the one gold object**, labelled with its own wordmark (Figure 2). Among red erythrocytes, white
  leukocytes and a pink platelet it is found before its label is read.
- **Each chapter makes one claim and closes on its summary.** Five chapters, each opening with a thin light-blue title,
  a bold blue one-line claim ("Cerebrolysin® stimulates neuroplasticity") and a QR code, with the key phrase of each
  paragraph in bold, and each ending on a full-bleed blue fact box of 3 or 4 one-line bullets. The five fact boxes
  alone carry the argument.
- **Restraint around loud renders.** One type family (Myriad Pro, light to black), one accent blue, text on white
  pages. The colour lives inside the pictures.

**Do not copy**

- **Its evidence standard.** It is the manufacturer's promotional booklet. Two of its 24 references are MedlinePlus
  and Wikipedia; a fact box says "Cerebrolysin® is unique and distinctive"; and preclinical work "demonstrated
  clearly" a result whose model (rats) appears only in the reference list. No evidence tier, no dashed arrow for an
  inferred step, no open question (moa-figures.md, "Status and evidence").
- **A mixture drawn as one molecule.** The text calls the drug "a parenteral biological drug consisting of peptides and
  amino acids"; the renders draw it as a single gold globular protein. A peptide mixture is many small bead strings
  (moa-figures.md, item 2).
- **A pathway told, not drawn.** The chain on p. 39 (VEGF, Ang1, Sonic hedgehog, miR-17-92, BDNF) lives only in the
  prose; the renders show cells, never the signalling steps between them (moa-figures.md, item 4).
- **Typos in print**: "Anti-inflammtory" in a chapter title and the contents, "Endothelia cell" in a label.
