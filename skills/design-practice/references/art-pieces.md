# Art pieces: pairs, wall text, drawings to scale

Part of design-practice: read when laying out a pair of pieces, writing a wall label, or drawing a piece to scale.

- **Twins mirror.** When two pieces form a pair (A and B covers, a diptych, a before and after), the second mirrors
  the first's layout: same heights, and the same alignment logic with the sides swapped. A shared right edge on A
  becomes a shared left edge on B, at the mirrored x (0.763 and 0.237). If one piece aligns and the other staggers, it
  reads as an error, so check the pair side by side. When the second piece's art gets in the way, move the text only as
  far as the first piece's measured gap between text and art requires, then mirror that edge back onto the first piece
  if the pair should stay exact. (a DJ tool covers 2026-10-20: "text position, should be mirrored a bit"; "i version not
  right aligned, ai version edge is left aligned?"; "i prefer the edge aligned look". B's name had to slide left to clear
  a spike; A's shared edge took the mirrored x.)
- **Wall labels follow museum convention, not the page's styling**: artist in bold (nationality and birth year if the
  artist gives them), title in italics with the year, medium, dimensions height x width x depth in inches and cm,
  edition, credit line; foreign terms in italics; no accent colour in the tombstone (the heading above it may keep its one
  accent word, "a choice" in gold, 2026-10-26) (an art piece wall text, 2026-10-26: "wall text
  need yellow on [the piece]? follow professional industry best standard practices - italics?").
- **Show scale with a figure drawn to scale and dimension lines**, beside a person of stated height, rather than notes
  like "approx. to scale"; measure the drawing first (a sink drawn 32 in tall beside a 5 ft 7 in viewer had to grow to
  its real 39 1/2 in). Reference artworks carry title, artist and year at a size that reads.
- **An about page wears its piece**: the piece's colours, shapes and density. A signal drawn there looks like the real
  instrument and is driven by the piece (its heartbeat as an EKG strip on black), and key parameter choices show as
  small animated previews rather than words (an open call's about page, 2026-11-19: "about page design should resonate
  design and style with the piece itself - colors, shapes, density"; "heartbeat @ bottom: black background, like real
  EKG, real life and linked to the piece"; "use animated preview for key parameter selections").
